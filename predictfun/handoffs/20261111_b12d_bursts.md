## Question

B12c: the top makers' edge is earned within 250 ms of the trade report and is not Binance. Three candidate
mechanisms on Predict's own data: (1) they pull right after being hit, before the follow-up taker orders; (2) they read
the book and avoid trades that come after the level thinned; (3) their size holds the price (they stay, and the level
survives their fills).

## Design

Same legs as B12b part A (55,514 at-best fills that did not go through the level, guard ON / OFF; top 5 10,656, ranks
6+ 44,858). Runs: taker orders on a side within 250 ms of each other. Re-hit: a maker that absorbed a whole trade at a
price was at the front with at least that size; under price-time matching, if it is still there it is filled first by
the next taker order at that price. So "hit again by the next order within 0.5 s" means it stayed. Thinning: the size
shown at the price 250 ms before the trade against just before it. Holds: the best price 250 ms after the trade is the
same or better for the maker. Readings R60-R64 and null N6 in the script header.

Controls, all passed before any table: B12b's controls; run positions and follow-up counts, re-hit opportunities
(same maker / another maker / another price / too late / shared order), thinning and holds on planted frames (bid and
ask), the null on a planted halved re-hit rate and on equal rates, the label rules. K0 reproduces b12.json and B12b's
part A legs.

## Post hoc: who the takers are (descriptive)

69% of the first-minute legs join one-to-one to a chain match record (our own id maps to our address; every signer id
maps to one address). Three of the five top makers also trade as takers: makers 48, 51 and 661 send 1.28%, 1.90% and
1.11% of the taker volume with a known taker; makers 30 and 40 never take. Makers hit by those taker orders lose -1.23
c/sh at 1 s (move -1.77), against +0.08 for all other taker orders. They hit ranks 21+ most: 8.3% of those makers'
fill shares at -1.61 c/sh (top 5 4.7% at -0.59, ranks 6-20 3.1% at -0.70, ours 1.9%). Without those taker orders the
within-guard gap is +0.16 c/sh instead of +0.21.

## What it says

1. They do not pull right after being hit; they stay more than anyone. After absorbing a trade, a top maker is still
   first in line for the next order at that price 59.5% of the time, against 38.4% for ranks 6+. Staying costs them
   nothing (+0.02 c/sh), while it costs the others (ranks 6+ -0.65 when they stayed against -0.34 when they left).
2. Where their fills sit in runs, and whether the level thinned before the trade, account for about a tenth of the gap
   each. The level holds only a little more often after their fills (+5 pp at the same reach). The declared label is
   HOLD-THE-PRICE because they stay; the holding itself is small.
3. Post hoc: about a quarter of the gap is the top makers' own taking. Three of them also take, their taker orders are
   the most toxic flow on the tape, and those orders land on other makers' quotes, mostly the slow tail (ranks 21+).
4. The losses are in stress moments. After the level thinned, ranks 6+ lose -1.04 c/sh and the top 5 -0.31; at stable
   levels the groups are close. At the best, ranks 6-20 do as well as the top 5; the losers are ranks 21+ and us.

What is left (about three quarters of the gap once their own taker orders are removed) is not visible on the public
tape: in the moments when Predict's book is under
stress, the trades that fill the top makers are followed by prices that hold, and the trades that fill the slow tail
by prices that break. Cancels are anonymous on the public tape, so how fast each maker updates its quotes when
Predict's book changes cannot be measured from it.

## What this changes

- For our guard: the signals are on Predict, not Binance: the level we are in thinning, and informed takers (some of
  them top makers) starting to hit. Our fills after the level shrank lose -5.83 c/sh (21% of our at-best shares, 28
  legs), and our level held after only 42% of our fills (top 5 82%).
- Our engine's reaction to Predict book changes is measured by B1 (live run with the A4b capture, USER D2), not by the
  public tape.
- The slow-cancel issue (B12b) stays with the live session.

## Limits

One consumed Sunday; point estimates. Trade times are reports. No re-hit cannot tell a cancel from an order used up
exactly; the top makers' larger orders make "used up exactly" rarer for them, which raises their re-hit rate a little
by itself. Holds after a fill is partly the outcome itself. The taker check is post hoc and uses the 69% of legs with an
unambiguous join. Our own rows are 28 legs (11 re-hit opportunities).
