The learned pull policies fail the primary trading-value/inventory objective against the matched guard control. They recover much of the loss from quoting without signal-based cancellation, but that is a weak benchmark: the unguarded default policy loses $2,643.22 at settlement. Its primary interval includes zero. These are computed from the report's `marked_daily` and interval fields, not a claim of robustness.

The pressure rule does not preserve the frozen ladder strategy's trading value: −$153.08 paired marked value against that reference (1,533 common marks; 195 missing), despite 94.119% less inventory-square time. The matched guard and other new arms share 5-share JOIN quoting, no ladder and reserved ±10 inventory; the frozen reference differs in size, ladder and inventory handling (observed maximum inventory 60 shares versus 10 in new arms). Therefore the large exposure reduction against the reference cannot be credited to a better pull model.

Spread alone would give the wrong impression. Unconditional default quoting collects $687.66 of instantaneous-mid spread but has −$2,488.55 / −$2,593.34 strict 1s / 5s fill markout. For the pressure rule the corresponding values are +$29.65 spread and −$76.76 / −$75.14 markout; matched guard has +$45.90 spread and +$50.14 / +$36.64 markout. These are alternative valuations, not additive P&L. Spread-known / missing generation counts are 90,279 / 4 (default), 42,778 / 0 (pressure), and 100,548 / 0 (matched guard); both-horizon markout-known / missing counts are 90,250 / 33, 42,776 / 2, and 100,544 / 4. Unfilled generations remain in these populations. The report retains every action status.

Pressure's mean absolute terminal inventory is 1.213 shares versus matched guard's 1.524, across 1,728 windows each. Inventory-square time and strict marked value end at 240.1 seconds; remaining inventory is carried toward settlement. Neither lower area nor a smaller six-day settlement standard deviation proves lower future P&L variance. Settlement and carry must remain separate from marked trading value, especially when mark availability differs.

The neutral model diagnostic does identify better pulls than matched random selection on 40,059 actions, but that local score does not transfer into a profitable sequential policy. It holds future portfolio reactions approximately fixed, uses a two-second label and a one-second reset wait, and is not realizable portfolio P&L. The live-style replay produces about 221–223 thousand generations per learned policy, with about 89.6–90.0% pulled by the model and a median simulated order life of 132 ms. These percentages divide model pulls by all generations, including refused/unfilled orders. The model first scores accepted orders at age ≥100 ms; after a pull it allows re-entry when the fixed cooldown expires. Repeated entry during an unresolved hazard and the different decision population are plausible next hypotheses, not an established attribution of losses. This readout does not change the model, cooldown or thresholds.

The pressure control evaluates its current imbalance/thinning/flow conditions before both retention and new entry and keeps extending its cooldown while pressure persists. Its apparent advantage therefore includes conditional re-entry; it is not evidence that unconditional new quoting with cancellation alone works. Adding queue features has not established incremental portfolio benefit over market + age in this test.

Primary execution assumptions are the −50 ms trade anchor, 40 / 22 / 100 ms place / cancel / notice delays and queue rule M. Stress replays use the predeclared slower timing and queue rule L. All fill/queue assumptions remain uncalibrated. The new model folds are chronological, but inherited guard predictions are LODO, so this is not a historically deployable full-strategy OOS result. Protected dates remain untouched. Next decision waits for the complete stress comparison and final artifact/model/action audit.
