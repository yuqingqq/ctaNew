## Question

B12 found that the top makers' fills are followed by smaller moves than everyone's at the same price, within our
guard's states, and that our fills have the worst moves. Is that their place in the queue, or a better / faster guard?
And why are ours worst?

## Input checks (before the script; structure only)

- Matching is price-time (FIFO)-like: 94.6% of the cohort's taker-order levels of 5 sh or less (85,711) fill exactly one
  maker leg; legs per level grow with size. Pro-rata matching would fill most makers at a ~300 sh level in every trade.
  So the queue decides whom a trade that stops at the level fills (R42 FIFO-LIKE, recomputed in the script).
- Book frames are ~51 ms apart in the first minute (p90 83 ms). Live's cancel round trip is p50 19 ms, p90 33 ms.

## Design

A, queue reach: at-best legs, guard ON / OFF at lag 50, trades that do not go through the level. Reach r = the taker
order's shares at the level / the size shown there in the frame before. Under price-time matching a maker filled by a
trade of reach r sat within the first r of the queue; a maker at the front is filled by trades of every reach. The
top-5 gap is decomposed over guard x reach cells: if it disappears within reach, it is queue place. B, exits: the top
makers' share of at-best fills before and after clean ON -> OFF flips of our guard. C, our 25 guard-OFF fills against
live's order paths: was the cancel already sent? Readings R40-R45 and nulls N2 / N3 in the script header.

Controls, all passed before any table: B12's seven, and A1-A3, B1-B2, C1, M1 and the NaN / R45 rules (planted queue
mix, planted within-reach skill, null with and without a planted skill, flip finder with flicker / gap / warm-up cases,
planted exits, planted late fills, planted FIFO and pro-rata fills). K0: the rebuilt frame reproduces the committed
b12.json (T1 for every group, the lag-50 decomposition) and B12's 25 own OFF legs / 160.0 sh exactly.

## What it says

1. Queue place is not why the top makers do better. At the same reach into the queue, they are filled by better trades
   (+0.23 c/sh, above the null). They are not nearer the front (median reach 0.213 against 0.181), and the trades that
   fill them are larger, not smaller (38 against 24 sh). At the best, ranks 6-20 do as well as the top 5 (+0.26 against
   +0.24 c/sh in these trades); the losers are ranks 21+ (-0.20), at the same queue places.
2. Our guard's value does not explain it either (post hoc). At the same guard value they still do better. Three
   quarters of the gap comes while our guard's value is falling, that is, while a move against the side is under way:
   then their fills lose -0.14 c/sh against -0.56, and fewer of their fills come then. They handle moves in progress
   better: they leave the level or reprice sooner, or they know which moves continue. The declared exit test could not
   separate the two (65 clean flips). In the user's terms: a better or faster guard for moves, not a better queue place.
3. Ours, two things. (a) While our guard is ON we sit further back than the top makers (median reach 0.30 against
   0.21, 28 legs) and make -0.25 against their +0.94 (B12). (b) Our first-minute loss on 11-08 is 11 fills on orders
   whose cancel we had already sent: the venue took 0.6-5 s to confirm those cancels, and the orders were filled in
   between. All 11 are 49-60 s into the window. Our other 14 guard-OFF fills net 0.00.
4. This corrects the B12 addendum's "pull timing first": our engine sent those pulls in time; the loss is the venue's
   slow cancels, concentrated 50-60 s into the window. That is a live / execution issue (below).

## What this changes

- The top makers' edge is a moves-in-progress problem: a faster or better read of sub-second moves than our guard's
  value carries. A queue tactic would not reach it. Signal or latency work aimed at moves under way (not at the guard's
  states) is where a B7-type change could pay; B1 (live run with the A4b capture, USER D2) measures our own reaction
  time order by order.
- Our losses: (i) the slow cancels go to the live session first (issue below); (ii) queue place: we are behind, and no
  rule tested buys priority at our latency (review 6.5).
- Rebate and rewards (D3, USER) are unchanged.

## Issue for the live session (Tokyo / Dublin): slow cancels 50-60 s into the window

- Evidence (11-08, tokyo-6 + basis-tokyo-1): 86 of 30,219 cancels took 0.5-5 s from request to ack (p50 1.1 s), 1.29% of
  those sent 50-60 s into the window against 0.06-0.20% elsewhere. No throttle or reject recorded. 11 of our
  first-minute fills came on orders inside such a cancel (all 49-60 s in): -4.88 c/sh, -$4.16 a day, our whole
  first-minute loss on the day.
- Mechanism: not known from these logs (venue or kernel). The kernel's cancel stages (kernel_orders: t_UserCancel,
  t_CancelPendingAudit, t_CancelQuantityReconciled, t_CancelAuditComplete) should show where the time goes.
- To verify: the same count on 11-07 (tokyo-5, a consumed day) and on any later run; the kernel stage times of the 86.
- Mitigation candidates (none tested): treat an order with a cancel pending as live risk until its ack; reduce size or
  stop quoting 50-60 s into the window if the pattern holds on other days.
