## 1. The numbers

### R515-R1 — HIGH — 333 cancels removed 1,440 fills. The reported per-cancel figure divides a whole-book delta by the wrong population. (CHECKED)

`cancellation_economics` reports `n_cancels` and nothing about fills. The fill
counts are one level down, in `arms[*].fields[*].n_fills`:

| arm | n_cancels | n_fills | **fills lost vs baseline** | fills per cancel | share of the book |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| QR_SKEW_ONLY (baseline) | 0 | **4,315** | — | — | — |
| CONDVALUE_X_SKEW | 333 | **2,875** | **1,440** | **4.32** | **33.4 %** |
| HAZARD_OVER_SKEWED_REF | 48 | **4,208** | **107** | **2.23** | 2.5 % |

`cents_per_cancel = net_pnl_delta / n_cancels` therefore attributes a delta
generated by **1,440 lost fills** to **333 cancel decisions**. The name is
literally accurate — it *is* cents per cancel — and it is the number a reader
will treat as the economics of a cancellation, which it is not. **Per lost fill
the figure is −0.6624 c, not −2.8646 c.**

This is also the arithmetic proof of a claim nobody had established: a cancel
does not remove one fill. The policy is stateful, and 4.32 fills per cancel is
the path effect, measured.

### R515-R2 — the corrected decomposition, and it CHANGES THE MECHANISM (CHECKED)

| quantity | baseline / fill | **per LOST fill** | ratio | per lost share | ratio |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| spread | 2.4489 c | **2.0372 c** | **0.832×** | 0.4989 c | 0.863× |
| adverse | −0.4561 c | **1.3748 c saved** | **3.01×** | 0.3366 c | 3.13× |
| net | +1.9928 c | **−0.6624 c** | — | −0.1622 c | — |

**The ranker is good, and that is the finding.** The fills it removes carry
**three times** the average adverse selection while carrying **less** than
average spread (0.83×). That is correct selection on both axes at once. On the
HAZARD arm the selection is sharper still — 3.81× adverse at 0.76× spread.

**It still loses, and the reason is the book, not the model.** The average fill
on this tape earns **+1.9928 c** (2.4489 spread against 0.4561 adverse), so
cancelling a *random* fill costs 1.99 c. Cancelling one of these costs 0.66 c.
**The policy is three times better than random at choosing what to decline and
is still declining profitable fills**, because on this book **adverse selection
is only 18.63 % of gross spread capture**. To profit from cancelling you must
find fills where adverse **exceeds** spread; these fills are not those fills.

### R515-R3 — I correct a reading of my own before filing it (CHECKED)

On `n_cancels` I computed the removed fills at **13.03× the average adverse and
3.60× the average spread**, and the arm's `adverse_saved` (1,979.66 c) at
**100.58 % of the baseline's entire net adverse selection** (1,968.19 c) — a
concentration I was about to report as extraordinary.

**On the correct denominator it is not extraordinary and it is fully
explained**: 33.4 % of the book removed at 3.01× the average adverse gives
0.334 × 3.01 = **1.006**, which is the 100.58 % exactly. The spread side
reconciles the same way: 0.334 × 0.832 = 0.278 against the reported 27.76 % of
spread forgone. **The 13× figure was an artefact of dividing by cancels, and
the 100.58 % is arithmetic, not a discovery.** Both numbers are internally
consistent; neither is evidence of a rare tail.

### R515-R4 — MEDIUM — `identity_holds` is an ALGEBRAIC TAUTOLOGY and cannot fail (CHECKED)

`de_phase4_diag_runner.maker_pnl_from_fills` (`:1213-1215`) defines:

```
spread  = sgn * (mid_at_fill    - level)
P&L     = sgn * (mid_at_markout - level)
adverse = P&L - spread
```

**`adverse` is a residual by definition**, so `maker_pnl ≡ spread + adverse` for
every arm, and the declared identity
`net_pnl_delta == adverse_saved − spread_forgone` follows algebraically from
`_forgone, _saved, _net = _b_sp − _sp, _ad − _b_ad, _pl − _b_pl`
(`de_section81_arms.py:969`). **The residuals — 6.8e-12, 6.8e-13, 1.8e-12 —
measure floating-point associativity, nothing else.**

The artifact's wording, *"COMPUTED per arm, never asserted"*, is true of the
computation and **misleads about its power**: it is SEAT_PROTOCOL 16's control
that cannot fail. It should be labelled as what it is — an arithmetic
round-trip — so that no reader takes it for a check that the decomposition is
economically complete. **It cannot double-count** (a residual cannot
double-count its own complement) and **it cannot detect an omission either.**

### R515-R5 — HIGH — the predicate printed beside the numbers describes an attribution the code does not perform (CHECKED)

> *"an arm pays SPREAD FORGONE on the fills it declines and is paid ADVERSE
> SAVED on the same fills"*

That describes a **per-fill attribution over a set of declined fills**. The code
computes a **difference of whole-book totals** between two replays. Those
coincide only if the arm's book is the baseline's book minus a set of fills and
nothing else — which R515-R1 refutes at the artifact: **333 cancels, 1,440
fewer fills.** Whatever else the suppression changed is inside `adverse_saved`
and `spread_forgone` too. This is CLAUDE.md pitfall 4 and DE36-R3's own
statement (*"a HELD side suppresses later crossings"*) showing up in the
economics summary.

The numbers are not wrong; **the sentence is**, and it is the sentence a reader
uses. The fix is one line: say *"the difference between two replays' book
totals, over a book that lost 1,440 fills"*, and lift `n_fills` and `shares`
into `cancellation_economics` where the deltas live.

**One check that PASSES and should be said**: exclusions are clean. All three
arms report `fill_statuses` `NO_MARKOUT` 0, `NO_MID_AT_FILL` 0, `NO_SHARES` 0,
with `VALUED` equal to `n_fills` (4,315 / 2,875 / 4,208). **No fill is silently
dropped from either side of the difference**, so my first hypothesis — that a
markout-exclusion asymmetry was masquerading as an avoided fill — is refuted at
the artifact. The counts exist one level down and are merely absent from the
summary, which is recoverable, not lost.

### R515-R6 — HIGH — the sign of −953.92 c cannot be assessed from what is emitted, and the machinery to assess it already exists in this repo (CHECKED)

`cancellation_economics` carries **no per-window breakdown**. 12 windows, 1,440
lost fills, delta −953.92 c ⇒ **−79.49 c per window** — a number nobody can
check, because the 12 values are not emitted.

**The bracket is wide enough that the question is open, not answered:**

* with fills independent (ρ = 0) and a per-fill P&L SD of ~5 c, SE ≈ 5·√1440 ≈
  190 c and the delta is **≈ 5σ**;
* with full within-window clustering the effective n is **12**, and the
  per-window SD is unmeasured, so the delta may be **1σ or less**.

The artifact is honest — *"12 windows is below the 5-complete-day cluster floor,
so a POINT ESTIMATE AND NO INTERVAL (rule 8)"* — but *no interval* is not the
same as *the sign is safe*, and the headline is a sign claim.

**This repo already contains the instrument.** `harmful_action_eval` emits a
`concentration` block on exactly this shape: `n_hours_net_positive`,
`max_single_hour_share_of_net`, `net_excluding_best_hour`,
`positive_without_best_hour` — built because *"a net figure that is really one
hour is not a robust effect, and the aggregate cannot show that."* **It is not
applied here.** Emitting the 12 per-window deltas costs one dict and answers the
question directly: if the sign survives dropping the worst window, it is a
finding; if it does not, it is one window wearing three significant figures.

### R515-R7 — the ceiling nobody has computed, and it bounds the whole overlay programme (CHECKED)

On this book **adverse selection is 18.63 % of gross spread capture**
(1,968.19 c against 10,566.95 c). A cancellation overlay can only ever recover
adverse selection, and only by paying spread. **The relevant ceiling is the sum
of |P&L| over fills whose P&L is negative** — the value a perfect oracle would
capture by declining exactly the losing fills and nothing else.

**That number is not emitted and it is one pass over the same fill records
`maker_pnl_from_fills` already walks.** It is the single most decision-bearing
quantity available here: if it is small against 8,598.76 c, the overlay's
ceiling is low **regardless of how good any model gets**, and that is a
programme-level fact rather than a model-level one.

## 2. Your item 2 — what has been optimised, and my prediction, before DE's

### What the programme has been optimising

**The smaller of the two terms, and the numbers say so.** Gross spread 10,566.95 c;
adverse selection 1,968.19 c — **18.63 %**. Market-making on this tape is not
adverse-selection-limited: the spread covers it 5.4× over, and the mean fill
earns +1.99 c.

**But "months wasted" is the wrong reading and the artifact does not support
it.** What the overlay research established — that a model can identify
adversely-selected flow — **is confirmed here, on absolute numbers, for the
first time**: 3.01× and 3.81× concentration of adverse selection at
below-average spread is real skill. What was never measured until now is
whether **acting** on that ranking pays, and the missing quantity was always
the spread the cancel forgoes. **Nothing in the Q1–Q3 head structure contains
it. Q4 is the head that contains it, and Q4 is the head that fails.** So the
programme has been optimising a ranking whose economic sign was never checked —
and the check has now been run twice, on two independent constructions
(iter011's Q4 increment, and this absolute decomposition), and agrees both
times.

### My expected direction for `inventory_loss_cents` — stated before DE's lands

**DIRECTION: it will FAVOUR cancelling. The delta moves UP from −953.92 c.**
Reasoning, and it is one step: the removed fills carry 3.01× the average
adverse selection, i.e. they are the fills where the market moved against the
maker. Adverse measured at `t + MARKOUT_S` is a *truncated* view of that move;
residual inventory carries whatever continues past the horizon. A position
selected for having moved against you is not, on average, a position that
recovers. **Confidence: moderate-high.**

**MAGNITUDE: I predict it does NOT flip the sign on this population.**
Confidence: low-moderate. The gap is **0.6624 c per lost fill** against
**1.3748 c** of already-measured adverse on the same fills, so inventory must
add **≈ 48 %** of the measured adverse to flip it — i.e. the markout horizon
would have to be capturing only about two-thirds of the total harm. Possible;
not my central case.

**AND THE FAILURE MODE I PREDICT, NAMED NOW SO A FLIP CANNOT BE EXPLAINED
AFTERWARDS.** These are 5-minute binaries. A residual position marked at
**settlement** pays 0 or 100 c per share; the entire spread/adverse ledger here
lives at **0.34–0.58 c per share** — two orders of magnitude smaller. **So if
`inventory_loss_cents` returns a number large enough to flip the sign, the
first thing to check is the MARK, not the model:** whether residual inventory is
valued at terminal mid (commensurate with everything else in this table) or at
binary settlement (not commensurate). R-506(E) already ruled the adjacent case —
a window ending in a gap yields `NOT_AVAILABLE`, never an in-codomain 0.5 — and
this is the same question one step further on.

**Pre-specified readings:**

| result | reading |
|---|---|
| favours cancelling by **< 0.66 c / lost fill** | my prediction confirmed; cancelling still loses; the binding quantity becomes R515-R7's oracle ceiling |
| **flips the sign** | check the mark first. A flip from settlement-marked residuals is a different quantity from a flip from post-markout drift, and only the second is evidence about the policy |
| **disfavours** cancelling | my reasoning is wrong: the declined fills' residual positions recovered, which would mean the markout horizon *overstates* their harm |

Two predictions now exist and they were made independently. **If DE's direction
matches mine, that is two observations; if DE's reasoning also matches mine, it
is one** — the same non-independence rule this programme adopted at R-508(D),
applied to a forecast.
