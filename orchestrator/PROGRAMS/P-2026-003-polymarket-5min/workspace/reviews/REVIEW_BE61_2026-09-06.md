## 1.1 What the stamp would — and would not — have caught for the 09-03 kill

**It would have caught nothing about that kill, and that is not a criticism.** The 09-03 smoke
died to an **external process-group TERM**: it never reached an emit, so nothing was stamped
and nothing could refuse. **Rule 22 is a provenance instrument, not a survival one.**

What it *does* catch is the DE 85 class (REV 49 §0): code replaced *under* a run that then
reaches its emit and would otherwise name bytes that did not produce it. That is the case it
exists for, and on these two receipts it is exercised for the first time on real artifacts —
`closure_unchanged_during_the_run: true` is now a measured fact about a 527 s and a 755 s run,
not a fixture's.

**The distinction is worth stating plainly** because the two arrived in the same week: **rule 22
makes receipts truthful; the launch form makes runs durable.** A reader who takes the new
stamps as protection against another lost run has conflated them — which is exactly the
confusion §4 shows is still live.

# 2. `dirty: true, dirty_paths: ['data']` — **accurate as a fact, misleading as a signal**

Both receipts record `worktree_was_dirty_at_import: true` with the ledger symlink as the only
entry. Confirmed at the source: `be_rule22.py` has **no exemption** for it — `grep` for
`symlink|exempt|data_link` returns nothing — and BE **records** the flag rather than refusing
on it.

* **As a fact it is true**: the worktree did carry an untracked `data` entry.
* **As a signal it is false.** The field exists so a reader can conclude *"the producing code
  may not be locatable in a commit."* Here that conclusion is wrong — I proved in §1 that both
  digests are the blobs at `eee7b3d3`. **The boolean is what a machine resolves; the
  disambiguation lives in a second field it has to know to cross-read.**
* **And the two seats now behave differently on the identical worktree state.** DE 94 hit this
  same condition and it **refused a real day** (`NOT_READY_P10_run_worktree_is_clean_at_import`);
  DE then exempted it *by property* and its receipts say `dirty_beyond_the_shared_data_link`.
  BE proceeds and says `dirty: true` with no exemption field. **A reader comparing a BE and a
  DE receipt from the same tree gets contradictory dirt statements.**

BE 62 carries the fix and it is the right one — classify by property, never by name. Until it
lands, **the honest reading of these two receipts requires cross-referencing `dirty_paths`
against the digest check**, and I would put that sentence in the register rather than leave it
to the next reader. *(One thing BE already has right: it parses `git status --porcelain` raw,
so it does not have the leading-space bug DE found in its own `.strip()`.)*

# 3. The resources — **"records the correlation, claims no mechanism" is right, and it understates what the receipts contain**

|  | 09-04 tape | 09-05 tape |
|---|---:|---:|
| rows | 638,602 | **489,434** (−23.4 %) |
| bytes | 1,165,058,495 | 890,640,737 |
| wall_s | 1,528.2 | **755.4** (−50.6 %) |
| `resources.peak_rss_gb` | **4.741** | **4.741** (identical) |
| `scope.peak_bytes` | **8,589,934,592** | 7,554,551,808 |
| `scope.max_bytes` | 8,589,934,592 | 8,589,934,592 |
| `scope.events.max` | **1,199** | **0** |
| `scope.anon_bytes` | 1,458,565,120 | 1,153,556,480 |
| `scope.file_bytes` | 3,514,445,824 | **3,645,927,424** (higher) |

**Refusing to assert a mechanism is right.** But two facts already in these receipts go past
correlation, and a reader who is only told "a correlation" will over-read the comparison:

1. **09-04's peak is EXACTLY its cap** — `peak_bytes == max_bytes == 8,589,934,592`, with
   `events.max = 1,199` reclaims that pinned it there. **That number is a bound, not a
   measurement of demand.** Comparing 8.590 GB against 09-05's 7.555 GB reads as "09-04 needed
   more"; what the receipts support is *"09-04's demand was at least the cap and was throttled
   1,199 times; 09-05's was 7.555 GB and was never throttled."* The two are not commensurable
   and the receipts say so if asked.
2. **The process peak is identical to three decimals on both days** — and I checked it is a
   real measurement, not a constant: `_rss_gb()` is
   `getrusage(RUSAGE_SELF).ru_maxrss` and the same field varies sensibly for the fragment
   builder across the same days (1.915 / 2.199 / 1.764). So across a **31 % row spread** the
   builder's own footprint does not move, the wall time does, and **the entire cgroup
   difference sits in `file_bytes` (page cache), which ROSE on the smaller day while
   `anon_bytes` FELL.**

Together those constrain any future mechanism without asserting one: **whatever differed was
outside the builder's own allocation.** That is stronger than a correlation, weaker than a
mechanism, and it is the sentence I would put in the receipt in place of the bare correlation.

# 4. The gating is evidenced; the lock is not — and **every BE heavy run is a `.scope`**

**The gating: CHECKED.** The tape receipt pins the fragment it consumed by digest —
`inputs.score_split.sha256 = 50318a3a7c011739…`, which is the fragment's own digest — with
`train_split` empty by construction and a `guard` that refuses any existing state-tape path.
So "the tape gated on the fragment" is verifiable from the artifact, by content.

**The lock: AGREED, not CHECKED.** Neither receipt mentions the heavy lock in any form —
`grep` for `heavy_run_lock`, `flock`, `lock_fd`, `.heavy_run.lock` and `wrapper` returns
**nothing** in either. The scope block carries the unit, the cgroup path, the caps and the
event counters, and no lock evidence at all. DE's runner records `wrapper_observed` with
`heavy_run_lock_held`, the holder pids and the flock modes, **and refuses a real day without
it**. So *"the lock was taken on the first attempt and held across both steps"* is a claim in
BE's row that its receipts cannot support. **One field would fix it**, and BE already imports a
module that could carry it.

**And the finding underneath: `unit` says `.scope` on every one.**

```
be58frag.scope  be58tape.scope  be61frag.scope  be61tape.scope
be55book.scope  be59book.scope (v1, .v2, .v3)
```

**Nine receipts, three days, every BE heavy run launched as a transient SCOPE** — the form
R-628 ruled against and DE 94 replaced, because a scope's payload sits in the launching
shell's process tree and dies with it. I measured that mechanism myself last round (the scope
payload's `PPid` was `claude`; my own TERM to its group killed my shell). BE's runs were 527 s,
755 s, 766 s, 1,528 s and 39 minutes long. **They survived; survival is not the property.** The
09-05 artifacts exist because nothing signalled the launcher — not because the launch form
protected them.

R-628's remedy lives in DE's `the_one_command`. **Nothing carries it to BE's three producers**,
and their own receipts are the evidence: the `unit` field is right there, and no seat is
reading it.
