## 1. THE ANCHOR IS THE INTERESTING PART, AND DE MEASURED ITS WAY TO IT

The ruling said "every unnamed member's **recorded digest** equals disk". **There is no
recorded digest for an unnamed member** — `unnamed` is `_expected − mods` where `mods` is
`producing_code.import_closure.modules`, so a member with a recorded digest is by construction
*named*. Applied literally the rule is unsatisfiable, and its own fallback ("absent still
refuses") would have refused every book with an unnamed member — the opposite of unblocking (a).

**DE hit exactly this and wrote down how they got past it:**

> *"WHERE THE RECORDED DIGEST COMES FROM, measured rather than assumed: for these members the
> receipt's own `import_closure.modules` map has NO entry — they were never imported — so the
> digest the receipt does carry for them is the one at its `builder_commit`, read from git.
> The receipt's recorded digests are TRUNCATED (24 hex on the real receipt), so the comparison
> is by prefix in whichever direction is shorter; an equality test would have refused every
> member."*

Both halves are things only a drive finds. **The anchor — the receipt's own `builder_commit` —
is sound and composes** rather than duplicating: that commit is admitted only as `EXACT` or as
a `DESCENDANT` whose declared build digests match, so the bytes it names are already inside
the frozen world, and the new check adds "and they have not moved since".

## 2. DOES IT WEAKEN IDENTITY? NO — AND HERE IS WHAT IT STILL DOES NOT REACH

It **adds** a comparison where there was none: today an unnamed member is neither checked nor
refused, and the caller admits it purely because its *name* is on a hand-typed lazy-import
list. After `71440be` the name no longer decides — the bytes do — and the hand-typed list is
explicitly **not widened** (the record carries
`ruled_lazy_exemption_NOT_WIDENED.outside_the_exemption`). That is strictly more identity, and
it replaces a typed name set with a measured property, which is REVIEW 123's rule.

**Two residuals, neither introduced here, both worth naming so the rule is not oversold:**

1. **Membership.** The digest answers *"have these bytes moved?"*, never *"should this module
   be in the scoring set at all?"*. The runner states this itself:
   `[MEMBERSHIP_LIMIT] THIS PREDICATE CANNOT DETECT A RECEIPT THAT UNDER-RECORDS ITS OWN
   IMPORT CLOSURE`, guarded on the producing side by rule 22, not here.
2. **Was the build's own copy the declared one?** The anchor is the receipt's builder commit,
   and the five *build*-pinned digests do not include scoring-set members — so "unchanged
   since the build" does not imply "the build's copy was declared". **Cheap closure, and I
   measured that it already holds:** DA's population freeze v15 declares
   `be_score_neutrality.py` at `a455191d6bceec7e` and disk matches. Comparing the **declared**
   digest as well is one lookup and turns the claim into "unchanged since the build *and* the
   build's copy was the declared one".

*(A third, latent: `compared_n_hex = min(len(recorded), len(disk))` has no floor. On both real
paths it is 24 or 64 hex, so nothing is vacuous today — I measured 64 on the live receipt — but
a pathologically short recorded value would silently shrink the comparison. A `>= 16` floor
refusing `UNNAMED_NO_DIGEST` costs one line, and the field is already in the record.)*
