# 1. **THE `.strip()` FINDING — one defect, two halves, three seats, none safe twice**

The defect needs **both** of these, and either alone is harmless:

* a **whole-output `.strip()`** on `git status --porcelain` (which eats the leading space of the
  **first line only**), and
* a **`line[3:]`** slice (which then takes one character too many).

**Driven on a crafted two-line porcelain output:**

```
raw:  " M live/pm_research/be_daybook_build.py\n?? data\n"

.strip() + [3:]        -> ['ive/pm_research/be_daybook_build.py', 'data']   ** one char short **
raw      + [3:]        -> ['live/pm_research/be_daybook_build.py', 'data']   ok
.strip() + [2:].strip()-> ['live/pm_research/be_daybook_build.py', 'data']   ok
```

**And the census of who has which half:**

| seat | the READ | the SLICE | safe because |
|---|---|---|---|
| DE `de_multiday_gate1_runner` | `raw=True` ✓ | `x[3:]` | **the read** |
| BE `be_rule22` | `raw=True` ✓ | `line[3:]` | **the read** |
| DA `da_book_verify` | `r.stdout.strip()` ✗ | `line[:2]` / `line[2:].strip()` | **the slice** |

**So the answer to "one shared helper or two copies of one defect" is neither.** It is one
defect written independently into two seats, of which **each seat has fixed the opposite half**
— DE and BE fixed the read and kept `[3:]`; **DA fixed the slice and kept the `.strip()`**, and
DA's own comment names the `[3:]` defect it declined to write, before either of the others
found the pair. *(DA's two other porcelain readers pass `-- <one file>` and test truthiness
only; they never slice a path, so the defect cannot reach them.)*

**The consequence, and it is the reason this is worth a register line:** **no seat is protected
by both halves.** BE and DE reintroduce the defect the day `raw=True` is dropped; DA
reintroduces it the day someone writes the natural `line[3:]` idiom — which is what both other
seats wrote. The durable fix is the **read**, because the slice is the half people edit.
