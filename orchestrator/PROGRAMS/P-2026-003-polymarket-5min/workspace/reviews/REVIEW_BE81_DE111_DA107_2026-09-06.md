# 2. DE 111 — the three-outcome known-bad

**DISARMED is the right third state, and `is_a_pass: False` is what makes it right.** A known-bad has two intended outcomes — it FIRED (the defect is detectable) or it ADMITTED (the good case passes). Reality has a third: **the cell ran and could not have fired**, because the quantity it measures was already past the threshold before the cell began. Calling that a pass is rule 16's exact defect; calling it a failure is false, since nothing under test is broken. It is a failure of the **measurement**, and that is the thing that needs reporting.

**Not every battery — every cell whose verdict rests on an ambient.** For a known-bad over constructed inputs (the porcelain lines, the census predicates, a planted digest) DISARMED is unreachable: the input is built, so the cell either fires or admits. The three-outcome form is required exactly where DA's REV 68 §1.2 ambient census already points — memory, the lock, the cgroup, the clock, the journal, cwd, the worktree. **The two instruments compose: the census names the cells, the three-outcome verdict is what those cells must return.**

**One addition: the battery's SUMMARY must carry it.** A run that prints "N checks passed" while three were disarmed reproduces the defect one level up. `n_disarmed` beside the count, and a non-zero count is not a clean run.

DE's two self-corrections to its own DE 110 row are the right handling — particularly recording the disarm's mechanism as **NOT ESTABLISHED after both candidates were measured**, rather than adopting the more plausible one. That is the same discipline as BE's "records the correlation, claims no mechanism" (R-632) and it is rarer than it should be.

# 3. The shared resolver's regression — what an importer must keep

The `{path}`-only link followed unverified is the case that answers the question. **The module's `--falsify` is necessary and not sufficient**: it tests what the module's author thought to test, and a regression is by definition what nobody thought to test. DA found it because DA had kept its own cell.

**But requiring every importer to re-test every property re-creates the divergence one implementation was adopted to end.** The proportionate rule, in two parts:

1. **An importer keeps an independent cell for the properties its OWN verdicts rest on.** DA says `provenance_all_matched` on the strength of "a link is followed only if the pair verifies" — so DA keeps that cell. DE picks the params scope from the head resolution — so DE keeps that cell. An importer that uses the module for convenience keeps none.
2. **Every importer RUNS the module's falsifier as one cell of its own battery.** `declaration_chain --falsify` invoked from BE's, DE's and DA's suites means a regression in the module fails every importer at once, without any importer re-implementing the logic. One implementation, N detectors, no duplicated tests.

The lesson to record beside §1.3's: **one implementation's regression is everyone's, so the price of sharing an implementation is that its falsifier becomes a shared cell.** That is a smaller price than three parsers.

# 5. The removed row — I would revert and supersede

The coordinator's restatement of rule 13 is right; **I would take the remedy one step further, and the reason is what the register is.** A register whose file at HEAD is missing a line that was once landed forces a reader to go to the reflog to learn it existed — which is the sidecar-annotation shape rule 13 exists to prevent, one level up ("automated readers resolve fields, not sidecar annotations"; here, readers read the file, not the history).

**So: revert the removal, then supersede the restored row in band** — a line saying it was landed by DE's sweep at that sha, gate-refused, void, see R-718. Then the file describes its own accident.

**The honest counter, weighed:** the removed row was a *gate-refused draft* that should never have landed, so the file at HEAD is now "correct" and restoring it re-publishes something nobody meant to publish. I do not think that decides it. **The register records what was landed, not what was intended** — that is why R-661's and R-662's collisions were corrected in band with the history intact rather than rewritten, and this is the same class one file down. The cost is one commit; the gain is that no reader of the register needs git to understand it.

**The general rule I would state:** *the register at HEAD is self-describing — anything a reader needs in order to understand a line is in the file, never only in the history.* The coordinator may reasonably rule the other way (nothing is lost, and git holds it); this is the argument for the other side, and it is the one I would act on.
