The two significant cells are concentrated; they are not a handful.
- **Regime concentration in time** — no. 20 of 24 hours contribute positively; the top three hours are 43% of the total (20Z 19%, 01Z 13%, 06Z 11%). Broad, not one episode.
- **Latency interaction at t+L** — no mechanism available. `value_cents` is resolved once per row at the frozen L = 50 ms and **both arms read the same row's value**; the arms differ only in which row they select. There is no arm-asymmetric latency term to hide in.
- **The 121 negative against 160 positive** — that is the shape of a broad positive-mean effect, and it is consistent with the volume account rather than against it: the candidate acts in far more windows, so it books both more wins and more losses.

**The 15% cell clears its multiplicity correction by exactly nothing, and would have failed on one seed in five.** The seed was pre-declared, so this is not seed-shopping — but the reported 0.04248 carries Monte-Carlo error of order ±0.017 on the adjusted value and must not be read as a measurement to five decimals. **The 10% cell is genuinely robust on this axis.** If the read is quoted as "two significant cells", it should be quoted as one robust and one marginal.

## (3) Is BY_COUNT's negativity trustworthy? — yes, and its defect cuts the other way

BY_COUNT reads its cutoff off the scored data, which is a real defect. But it grants that retrospective advantage to **both arms symmetrically**: each cancels its own top-kk by its own ranking on the day being scored. If a retrospective cutoff flatters anyone it flatters the arm with the better in-sample ranking — which is the arm under test. The candidate loses anyway, at all three budgets.

And the matched-volume counterfactual gives the incumbent **no** retrospective advantage — its theta is set to reproduce the candidate's realised count, not to optimise anything — and reaches the same sign at all three budgets. So BY_COUNT's negativity is not an artifact that weakens your conclusion; it is corroborated by a construction that does not share its defect.
